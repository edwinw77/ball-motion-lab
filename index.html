Boards count from the right gutter (1) to the left (39). With the release empty, each ball gets its optimal line (opt): it hits the pocket with an entry angle as close to 6° as possible, breakpoint at board 4 or further left. With a release set, * means it was moved left so the ball stays off the right gutter. Same line throws every ball on one shared line, the average of their own lines (same), so differences show directly. Hook shape is how far the ball travels while making most of its turn: under 24 ft angular, 24–28 ft medium, 28 ft and over arc.

How the model works
  • The ball is a rigid sphere sliding and spinning on the lane. Friction acts against the slip at the contact point. It slows the ball, turns it left (the hook) and converts spin until the ball rolls; then the path straightens.
  • RG sets the moment of inertia (I = m·RG²). Lower RG converts to roll sooner (earlier, smoother read); higher RG goes longer before hooking.
  • Differential (plus intermediate differential for asymmetric balls) stands in for flare. More flare means more hook. Asymmetric balls also respond more sharply where the oil ends. These are calibrated factors, not full core dynamics.
  • Coverstock and grit set friction in oil and on dry boards, and how friction rises as the oil thins. Solid, hybrid and sanded surfaces start gripping as soon as the oil thins (earlier read, smooth arc). Pearl, finely sanded and polished surfaces stay slick until the boards are nearly dry, then grab hard (skid, then snap). Polish also lowers friction in the oil (more length); a factory compound counts as half a polish. Multi-step finishes use the final grit.
  • Asymmetric cores (intermediate differential) make the response sharper, so the motion is more angular.
  • Weight on its own barely changes the path (mass cancels out of the friction equations). Weight comparisons differ because each weight has different RG and differential.
  • Oil is fresh and thins gradually through the midlane before ending. House patterns have less oil on the outside boards; the sport pattern is flat across.
  • Not modelled: layout (pin-to-PAP, drilling angle), which in real life can make the same ball smoother or more angular. A neutral layout is assumed.
